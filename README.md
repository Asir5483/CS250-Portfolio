# CS-250 Portfolio

This portfolio contains work from my Computer Science program, including my Sprint Review and Retrospective project from CS-250. The project helped me understand how Agile and Scrum practices can be used during software development.

## How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I think the best way to understand what a user needs is to make sure I understand what they are asking for before I start writing code. On the SNHU Travel project, the user stories helped break the website's features into smaller pieces, such as finding top destinations, searching by price, and viewing vacation deals. This made it easier to understand what each feature was supposed to do and what the customer expected from it. I also learned that asking questions and getting clarification is important because requirements can change. User stories help keep development focused on what the customer actually needs instead of making assumptions about what they want.

## How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I approach development by breaking a larger problem into smaller tasks that are easier to understand and work through. Throughout this course, I learned how Agile and Scrum help teams organize their work, review progress, and make changes when needed. In my future development work, I would like to use practices such as sprint planning, daily check-ins, backlog refinement, and sprint reviews. I also see the value of testing features against clear acceptance criteria before considering them complete. The biggest lesson for me is that development does not always go exactly as planned, so being able to adjust and communicate with the team is just as important as writing the code.

## What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

To me, being a good team member means communicating clearly, listening to other people's input, and being willing to ask questions when something is unclear. During the SNHU Travel project assignments, I learned how important it is for the developer, tester, and Product Owner to agree on what a feature should do before too much work is done. If everyone makes different assumptions, it can lead to mistakes and extra work. A good team member should take responsibility for their work, help others when possible, and be open to feedback. I also think it is important to be flexible when requirements change and to remember that the goal is for the whole team to deliver a product that works for the customer.
